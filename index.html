    1. Sun position (Spencer / NOAA) 
    γ = 2π(N−1)/365                                   N = day of year
δ = 0.006918 − 0.399912cosγ + 0.070257sinγ − 0.006758cos2γ + 0.000907sin2γ − 0.002697cos3γ + 0.00148sin3γ     (declination, rad)
EoT = 229.18×(0.000075 + 0.001868cosγ − 0.032077sinγ − 0.014615cos2γ − 0.040849sin2γ)                         (equation of time, min)
solar time = clock time + 4×(longitude − 15×timezone) + EoT     hour angle ω = 15°×(solar time − 12)
s = (E, N, U): E = −cosδ·sinω,  N = sinδ·cosφ − cosδ·cosω·sinφ,  U = sinφ·sinδ + cosφ·cosδ·cosω
elevation h = asin(U), azimuth A = atan2(E, N), clockwise from north
    2. Sunrise, sunset & altitude correction

    cosω₀ = (sin h₀ − sinφ·sinδ) / (cosφ·cosδ), h₀ = −(0.833° + 0.0293·√altitude). The 0.833° term is atmospheric refraction; the altitude term is the horizon dip (earlier sunrise, later sunset). Higher latitudes mean more extreme seasonal swings; if cosω₀ leaves [−1,1] you get polar day or polar night.

    3. Drying geometry & sunlit area

    The quilt is a rectangle L × W draped over the line with folded thickness t. The crease runs parallel to the line; the fold point splits the "edge perpendicular to the line" into f and (1−f), so the two hanging depths are f·D and (1−f)·D (D = length of the perpendicular edge).

    The two layers shade each other; at any instant the sunward "silhouette" is governed by the longer layer (with an under-edge leakage approximation). The instantaneous effective sunlit area is:

    A(t) = S × [ Lmax × cos h(t) × |cos(A(t) − β)| + t × sin h(t) ]
S = length unfolded along the line (L if the long edge is on the line, W otherwise)
Lmax = max(f, 1−f) × D        β = line azimuth ± 90° (the two layer normals)
The first term is the projected light on the panels; the second is light on the crest over the line
    4. Fold decision (scheme search) & safety constraints

    The tool treats the along-line edge and the fold fraction as decision variables and searches all combinations for the highest daily exposure:

    scheme = (edge along line ∈ {long, short}) × (fold = big-side fraction f ∈ [0.5, 0.9])
exposure G(scheme) = S × [ f × D × Icos + t × Isin ]      (S and D follow the chosen edge)
constraint 1 · friction: f/(1−f) ≤ κ/1.3     (κ=e^(μπ), 1.3 = safety factor)
constraint 2 · clearance: f × D ≤ line height − 0.06 m (lowest point at least 6 cm above ground)
constraint 3 · line length: S + 0.2 m ≤ available line (tying margin included)
Among all constraint-satisfying schemes, the largest G is recommended

    A notable property: at the same fold fraction, "long edge on the line" and "short edge on the line" collect almost the same total light (both equal fold fraction × quilt area); only the crest length differs. The real differences are the line length required and the hanging-depth limit. With a long line and a low line height the long edge usually wins (the shallower drop allows a more asymmetric fold); with a short line, hang the short edge.

    Why is the recommended fold almost always 66:34? The sunlit area is proportional to the longer layer's drop length (with the two layers mutually shadowing, the silhouette decides), so more asymmetry always means more light — the optimum therefore sits at the maximum the constraints allow: with the defaults (μ=0.30 plus a 1.3× safety margin) that is the friction limit of 66.4%. It does not change with location, date or line direction — the sun only decides which side the longer layer faces, and the value of G. It moves only if you change the friction μ, or lower the line height until the ground-clearance constraint binds first.

    Orientation verdict (morning / afternoon): for a north–south line the two sides are theoretically exactly symmetric (0.0 min apart when due N–S); for an east–west line the gap is huge (hours). Rule: if the two lit integrals differ by less than 0.6 h (the scale of compass error and magnetic declination), the sides are declared "equivalent" and no orientation is forced; 0.6–1.5 h is reported as "slightly better"; only larger gaps get a firm verdict. Phone compasses read magnetic north, which may differ from true north by a few degrees — this rule is deliberately desensitized to that.

    5. Exposure score (area × time)

    G = ∫ A(t) dt(sunrise to sunset, unit m²·h) accumulates "how much area is sunlit at each moment" over time — the total sunlit area-time. The on-screen score = current G ÷ the best G* reachable that day by turning the line × 100.

    Note: the score uses geometric exposure (cosine-weighted area × time); the energy estimate (MJ) additionally weights beam power and low-sun atmospheric attenuation — same trend, different magnitudes.

    6. Uniform-mass assumption & "will it slip"

    The quilt is treated as uniform in mass: the weight ratio across the crease is f : (1−f). The fabric wraps the line over about π radians; under the capstan friction model the no-slip condition is:

    T₁/T₂ ≤ e^(μ·π)   →   f/(1−f) ≤ κ, κ = e^(μ·π)   →   fold limit f ≤ κ/(1+κ)

    At the default μ = 0.30 the theoretical limit is about 72:28; the recommended fold keeps a 1.3× safety margin (about 66:34). In practice, wrap anti-slip tape at the crease or add clothespins to further reduce slipping.

    7. Energy estimate (air mass & beam intensity) 
    AM = 1 / [cos z + 0.50572·(96.07995 − z)^(−1.6364)] × (P/P₀), z = zenith angle
P/P₀ = (1 − 2.25577×10⁻⁵ × altitude)^5.25588
DNI = 1353 × 0.7^(AM^0.678) × kc      (W/m², kc = sky clarity)
Q = ∫ DNI(t) × A(t) dt                (energy, assuming full absorption)
    8. Location, altitude & compass reading

    Latitude, longitude and altitude describe the drying site and determine the sun's path (elevation, azimuth, sunrise/sunset, atmospheric attenuation). They have nothing to do with the quilt's long or short edge.

    The compass reading (e.g. 90°) only describes the rope's orientation. Which quilt edge lies along the line and where to fold are "how to dry" decisions this tool computes (top conclusion); you can also pin an edge in the sidebar for comparison. Hold the phone level, sight along the line and read; pointing the opposite way gives a reading 180° apart, which is equivalent.

    9. Assumptions & limitations
    • Direct sunlight only. Diffuse sky light, ground reflection, clouds, humidity and wind are not modeled — they strongly affect real drying speed on overcast and cold days.
    • The quilt is idealized as rigid flat panels, ignoring bulging, draping curvature and layer contact; mutual shading uses a silhouette approximation with under-edge leakage.
    • No buildings, trees or railings; assumed open ground with no reflection.
    • Energy assumes full absorption (no fabric reflectance) — magnitude reference only.
    • The friction model is an idealized uniform-contact capstan approximation; real rope and fabric vary, and wind load is not included.
    • Results compare drying methods, windows and seasons — they are not a measured drying time.
